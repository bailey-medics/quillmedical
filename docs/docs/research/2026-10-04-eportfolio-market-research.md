# Clinician Passport enters a fragmented, mostly sponsored market

UK clinicians already have plenty of portfolio tools. Almost every one of them belongs to a curriculum owner, college, professional body, employer or staffing platform, and the clinician seldom pays for it directly. Training ePortfolios (Horus, Turas, risr/advance, ISCP, the RCGP portfolio on FourteenFish) are mandated and either free or bundled into a college fee. Non-medical professions mostly get revalidation and CPD tools free through membership or the NHS. The paid direct-to-clinician market exists but is cheap. Nurse, AHP and pharmacy apps cost about £15 to £36 a year, and GP appraisal portfolios cost about £35 to £185 a year. B2B products such as Compassly and Patchwork are paid for by trusts. None of the products reviewed gives a doctor a single record they own and carry across employers and training stages, with competencies, sign-offs, logbook, reflections, CPD and certificates in one place. That is the space Clinician Passport would occupy. The price is the weak point. At £9.99 a month (£119.88 a year) it would cost three to eight times the going rate for comparable individual tools, while at £9.99 a year it would undercut every paid competitor and bring in very little revenue. For a doctor-focused addressable market of about 166,000, the gap between those two readings is £19.9m against £1.66m a year at full penetration. The oncology beachhead in Cheltenham gives the product credibility but will not generate meaningful revenue, and oncology nurses and pharmacists already have a free, nationally endorsed competency passport. The main risks are naming (several products already use "passport"), the legal discoverability of reflections, which a git history makes harder to manage, and the fact that several key prices rest on one third-party source that sells a competing product.

## Mandated systems and membership bundles hold most clinicians

### Doctors in training have no choice of platform

Training portfolios are chosen by the curriculum owner, so trainees cannot pick a product. In England, Horus is built and run by NHS England for foundation doctors, non-training-grade doctors and supervisors. It is free, and "it is not possible to pay to use the Horus ePortfolio" ([Horus FAQs](https://supporthorus.hee.nhs.uk/faqs/)). Scotland and Wales use NES's Turas Portfolio ([Turas Portfolio](https://turasportfolio.nes.nhs.scot/); [Turas Wales](https://turasportfoliowales.nes.digital/)). Most medical specialties use **risr/advance, formerly Kaizen**, including the RCR, RCEM and RCPCH in the UK and RCPI and the College of Anaesthesiologists in Ireland ([RCR](https://www.rcr.ac.uk/exams-training/specialty-training/risradvance-formerly-known-as-kaizen/); [RCPI](https://help.rcpi.ie/support/solutions/articles/43000513618-risr-kaizen-video-guide-for-trainees); [CAI PCS guide 2024](https://www.anaesthesia.ie/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PCS-Risr-Advance-Kaizen-Guidelines-for-Users-2024.pdf)). risr/ is a London-headquartered B2B vendor and claims more than 100 medical institution clients, including the GMC and the Royal College of Physicians ([risr/](https://risr.global/company/)). Trainees pay through their college. **RCEM includes ePortfolio access in membership**, and non-members must buy a membership category that includes it ([RCEM](https://rcem.ac.uk/eportfolio-access/)). Surgical trainees pay a **JCST fee of £260 a year**, which funds ISCP, and if they do not pay, the interactive areas are locked ([JCST FAQs](https://www.jcst.org/uk-trainees/str-trainees/jcst-fee/faqs/)). The RCGP moved 25,000 active users and almost three million portfolio items to FourteenFish in 2021 ([RCGP](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/blog/new-appraisal-portfolio)). That shows platform changes are large migrations controlled by the college, with no say for the individual.

This fragmentation is the clearest structural pain point. An ACCS trainee may work in Kaizen for emergency medicine, the JRCPTB portfolio for acute medicine and the RCoA Lifelong Learning platform for anaesthetics ([NHS England LaSE](https://lasepgmdesupport.hee.nhs.uk/support/solutions/articles/7000022531-what-eportfolio-should-i-use-)). Export is mainly PDF. RCEM allows bulk report downloads and per-event PDFs with a 4MB limit ([RCEM](https://rcem.ac.uk/eportfolio-access/)), and data from the pre-Horus foundation portfolio was archived behind a separate retrieval process ([Horus archive](https://supporthorus.hee.nhs.uk/about-horus/access-to-foundation-information-held-in-previous-e-portfolios/)). The research found no structured, interoperable export standard and no GMC or NHS API for third-party portfolios. Importing into Clinician Passport will therefore mean parsing PDFs unless APIs can be negotiated.

### Consultants, SAS doctors and GPs split between employer licences and individual purchase

Secondary-care consultants and SAS doctors mostly use appraisal systems licensed by their employer, such as L2P, SARD and Allocate, so they lose access when they move. **L2P sells an individual "continuing to use L2P" subscription**, and l2p.co.uk now redirects to Patchwork Health ([L2P subscriptions](https://www.l2p.co.uk/doctor/subscriptions)). The price could not be retrieved. Still, the subscription shows that a vendor already charges doctors who leave an organisational licence, which is the scenario Clinician Passport is built for. Independent appraisal providers also sell L2P-based revalidation to doctors without a designated-body employer ([ACI](https://www.acitrainingandconsultancy.co.uk/revalidation-services/doctors-revalidation-with-l2p-appraisal-software)). The physicians' Federation CPD diary is free to RCP, RCPE and RCPSG members and **£165 a year for non-members** ([Federation CPD diary](https://www.thefederation.uk/cpd/cpd-diary-overview)). RCP collegiate membership for consultants costs £190 to £260 a year ([RCP rates](https://www.rcp.ac.uk/media/yoqphi34/membership-rates.pdf)).

GP appraisal is the one competitive market where individuals choose and pay. In September 2026 the reported prices were **FourteenFish £49.99 a year** (£34.99 for RCGP members, free for eligible First5 GPs), **Agilio Clarity £99, £130 or £185 plus VAT** depending on tier (£30 plus VAT for doctors within five years of qualifying), **GP Tools at £39.99** after a free first year, and **Umbil Pro at £24 a month**. BMJ Portfolio is free ([iatroX](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/cheapest-gp-appraisal-portfolio-2026-real-cost-psq-msf)). All of these 2026 figures come from iatroX, which sells its own AI CPD tool, so they need confirming with each vendor before anyone quotes them. FourteenFish's own historic prices (£42 in 2015 and in 2021, with 30% off for RCGP members) roughly match ([FourteenFish 2015](https://www.fourteenfish.com/blog/newprices); [RCGP 2021](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/blog/new-appraisal-portfolio)). When a practice buys Clarity licences, the individual GP pays nothing.

### Non-medical professions pay little or nothing

NMC revalidation every three years requires 450 practice hours, 35 CPD hours, five pieces of feedback, five reflective accounts, a reflective discussion and confirmation ([Logbook.co.uk](https://www.logbook.co.uk/nmc-revalidation-uk-nurses-complete-guide/)). The tools for it are cheap. **RCNi Portfolio is free to RCN members** ([RCN Learn](https://rcnlearn.rcn.org.uk/Portfolio)). FourteenFish charges £15 a year for its nurse revalidation toolkit ([FourteenFish](https://www.fourteenfish.com/audience/nurseormidwife)), and Revalidate charges £2.99 a month for premium ([Revalidate](https://www.revalidateapp.com/)). The HCPC sets no CPD hours, but registrants must keep a continuous record and about 2.5% are audited at each renewal ([CSP](https://www.csp.org.uk/frontline/article/hcpc-audit-are-you-ready)). Professional bodies bundle the tools. RCOT provides CPDme premium as a member benefit ([RCOT](https://www.rcot.co.uk/discover-membership/your-cpd/cpd-portfolio)), the SoR has CPD Now, and the RCSLT is replacing its diary in 2026 after members asked for better mobile use, more flexible export and automated reminders ([RCSLT](https://www.rcslt.org/news/were-developing-a-new-and-improved-cpd-diary-for-members/)). CPDme covers more than 120 CPD standards and sells to individuals at **£2.99 a month or £24.99 a year** ([CPDme](https://www.cpdme.com/)). Pharmacists revalidate annually through myGPhC ([GPhC](https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/pharmacists/revalidation-renewal)), and foundation trainee pharmacists use an NHS England e-portfolio built by Axia Digital ([Axia](https://www.axiadigital.uk/customer/nhs-england/)). Dentists log CPD over a five-year cycle, often through content bundles such as Dentaljuce at £89 a year ([Dentaljuce](https://www.dentaljuce.com/regular-membership)). Experienced ACPs can use the NHS England Centre for Advancing Practice ePortfolio route, which is fully funded ([Centre for Advancing Practice](https://advanced-practice.hee.nhs.uk/our-work/eportfolio-route/)). PAs and AAs have been GMC-regulated since 13 December 2024, but their revalidation model was still being developed when last checked ([GMC](https://www.gmc-uk.org/news/news-archive/regulation-of-physician-associates-and-anaesthesia-associates-begins)).

Pre-registration nursing evidence sits in systems licensed by universities. The 14-university Pan London consortium, with about 10,000 students, uses MyKnowledgeMap MyProgress for its electronic Practice Assessment Document ([MyKnowledgeMap](https://www.myknowledgemap.com/post/myprogress-and-pan-london-consortium)). PebblePad offers free alumni accounts that universities create for leaving students ([PebblePad help](https://help.pebblepad.com/helpfile.aspx?f=271&t=1&tg=3bf540bf-f5a7-4714-a00e-bca93111676b&v=16&vn=5.0)). Continuity after qualification depends on the institution.

## Pricing arithmetic: the billing period is the business case

"£9.99 per period" covers a twelvefold range of revenue. The table below uses the registrant counts in the research: GMC licensed doctors (end 2024), PAs and AAs (September 2025), NMC (March 2026), HCPC (September 2026), GPhC (September 2026) and GDC dentists (January 2026) ([GMC Workforce report 2025](https://www.gmc-uk.org/cdn/documents/wfr25-report-251117_pdf-112967442.pdf); [NMC](https://www.nmc.org.uk/about-us/reports-and-accounts/registration-statistics/); [HCPC](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/data/the-register/register-summary/); [GPhC](https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/about-us/publications-and-insights/research-data-and-insights/gphc-registers-data); [GDC](https://www.gdc-uk.org/news-blogs/news/detail/2026/01/19/gdc-register-increases-to-over-47-000-dentists)). The doctor SAM is 79,171 doctors in training plus 87,151 doctors on neither register (SAS and locally employed). The oncology figure adds 1,213 clinical oncology consultants, 582 clinical oncology trainees and about 650 medical oncology consultants (WTE used as a proxy for headcount) ([RCR 2024 census](https://www.rcr.ac.uk/media/empjepti/rcr-2024-clinical-oncology-workforce-census-report_.pdf)). The data dates differ. That is acceptable for sizing but should be footnoted in any investor material.

These are ceilings at 100% uptake. At a more realistic **1% of the doctor SAM (about 1,663 users)**, annual revenue is about £199k at the monthly price, £66k at the quarterly price and £16.6k at the annual price. The planned three months free also reduces first-year revenue under monthly billing to nine paid months, £89.91 per retained user, before churn. Monthly billing invites cancellation after each appraisal. Annual billing matches how the incumbents charge and how appraisal and revalidation cycles run.

Set against the comparators, the monthly reading puts Clinician Passport near **Clarity Pro (£185 plus VAT)** and the **RCP non-member diary (£165)**. It would be about **3.4 times Logitbox (£34.99)**, about **2.5 to 3.5 times FourteenFish**, about **six times CPD Passport (£19.99)** and about **five times CPDme (£24.99)**. For nurses and AHPs, £119.88 a year comes close to the regulator fee itself: the NMC rises to £143 from 1 October 2026 ([Sessional](https://sessional.co.uk/blog/nmc-changes-2026-locum-nurses-midwives)) and the HCPC is £123.34 ([HCPC consultation](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/globalassets/consultations/2026/hcpc-fees/consultation-on-hcpc-fees-2026---consultation-document.pdf)). The quarterly reading (£39.96 a year) sits inside the GP appraisal band and slightly above Logitbox, and on present evidence it is the most defensible point for doctors. The annual reading is an impulse price that will not cover support and acquisition costs, however low the running costs. Comparators differ on VAT (Clarity quotes ex-VAT, FourteenFish's 2015 price was VAT-inclusive), so the published price should say which it is.

Tax relief does not help much. Employed clinicians can claim relief on fees to bodies on HMRC List 3, but an employee's software subscription must meet the "wholly, exclusively and necessarily" test ([Goforma](https://www.goforma.com/medical-accountants/healthcare-worker-tax-relief-expenses)). That is hard to show when an employer or college already supplies a portfolio. Self-employed locums are more likely to be able to claim it as a business expense, which is one more reason to target them.

## The gap is a doctor-owned record that spans employers

Each existing product covers part of the record. Training portfolios hold WPBAs but are owned by the college or NHS body and export only to PDF. Appraisal tools hold one cycle's supporting information and are often employer-licensed. Locum and bank platforms (Patchwork, Locum's Nest, Lantum) hold employment-check documents inside their own networks, paid for by trusts ([Patchwork G-Cloud](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/143920545405909)). Compassly holds competencies for non-medical oncology staff. CPD Passport, CPDme and Revalidate hold CPD. Logitbox holds a logbook. The national Digital Staff Passport only ever covered identity, right to work, DBS, registration and occupational health ([UKAuthority](https://www.ukauthority.com/articles/nhs-digital-staff-passport-hits-stability-issue/)). None of them gives a doctor one structured record they own, that persists across rotations, colleges, employers and the move from training to consultant or locum work, and that holds competencies, sign-offs, logbook, reflections, CPD and certificates together. Clinician Passport's git-backed, file-per-item design fits this gap well. Clinician ownership and structured (non-PDF) export are the points of difference no incumbent offers.

The best-fit users are the clinicians with no sponsoring system or the most moves between systems. They include **87,151 doctors on neither GMC register**, among them 38,394 locally employed doctors and 12,577 SAS doctors. Locums are also a strong fit, as are the **20,060 non-UK graduates** who took up licences in 2024 and doctors leaving training ([GMC Workforce report 2025](https://www.gmc-uk.org/cdn/documents/wfr25-report-251117_pdf-112967442.pdf)). Physician associates are a smaller, newly regulated group with no single incumbent, but their political position is uncertain. Trainees can use the product only as a personal mirror alongside the mandated system, since the curriculum owner will not give up the system of record.

Oncology needs a careful reading. The **UKONS SACT Competency Passport on Compassly** is free to UKONS members, nationally endorsed and recordable on ESR. It is aimed at nurses, therapy radiographers and pharmacists, and Compassly claims nearly 10,000 users across more than 100 NHS organisations ([UKONS](https://ukons.org/news/ukons-digital-sact-competency-passport); [NHS Innovation Accelerator](https://nhsaccelerator.com/innovations/compassly/)). Oncologists themselves appear unserved by any oncology-specific passport, so the Cheltenham doctor pilot does not collide with it. Extending to oncology nurses or pharmacists would, and import from or interoperability with Compassly would then matter. Compassly's model is also instructive. Trusts pay £1.50 to £3.50 per user per month, a sponsoring society pays for its own members, and individuals keep their login after the contract ends ([G-Cloud](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/110655381093440)). A hybrid of that kind, with a free clinician-owned core and revenue from trusts, deaneries or specialty societies paying for assessor workflows and framework hosting, matches market norms better than a consumer subscription alone.

AI-assisted reflection will not differentiate the product. BMJ Portfolio (free), Clarity's Leo AI, Umbil and Revalidation Copilot already offer it ([iatroX comparison](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/gp-appraisal-tools-compared-clarity-vs-fourteenfish-vs-ai-cpd-2026); [Revalidation Copilot](https://www.revalidationaicopilot.co.uk/blog/best-app-nmc-revalidation-2026.html)).

## Risks: naming, reflections and thin evidence

**Name confusion.** "Passport" is crowded. The NHS Digital Staff Passport was a national programme with £2m of interim support spending ([UKAuthority](https://www.ukauthority.com/articles/nhs-digital-staff-passport-hits-stability-issue/)). **CPD Passport (cpdpassport.co.uk)** is a live UK CPD log aimed at nurses and pharmacists ([CPD Passport](https://cpdpassport.co.uk/)). Compassly brands itself "Competency Passport" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/compassly-competency-passport/id1558081181)). Locum's Nest sells PassportPlus, Patchwork offers "digital staff passporting" and QuietMedical has a "Locum Compliance Passport". The current trade mark filing is for QUILL MEDICAL, so "Clinician Passport" works as a descriptive sub-brand with little protection of its own. A trust buyer may also assume "the NHS already has a passport". Ask the trade mark attorney for a clearance view on the sub-brand, and consider leading with "Quill" in the product name.

**Uncertain Digital Staff Passport status.** The research notes disagree. One source describes the DSP as still being built, with a £1.99m technical services contract in January 2024 ([HTN](https://htn.co.uk/2024/01/31/1-99m-contract-awarded-for-technical-services-to-support-nhs-digital-staff-passport-development/)). Another, a blog by competitor Credentially, says it was **retired on 5 December 2025** after reaching four trusts, with no replacement named ([Credentially](https://www.credentially.io/blogs/digital-staff-passport-nhs-uk-retired-replaced-or-still-missing)). The NHS England Digital announcement page returned 404 ([NHS England Digital](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/digital-staff-passport/news/important-announcement-about-the-future-of-nhs-digital-staff-passport)). Treat the retirement as probable but unconfirmed. Either way, the DSP never covered competencies or CPD, and NHS England's StatMand programme had relied on it for portable training records ([NHS England StatMand](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/statutory-and-mandatory-training-programme/)). That leaves an opening, although trusts will still trust ESR Inter-Authority Transfer over a record the clinician holds.

**Reflection discoverability.** E-portfolio reflections are "not subject to legal privilege under UK criminal law" and could be requested by a court. After Bawa-Garba the LMCs Conference voted for GPs to disengage from written reflections ([Pulse](https://www.pulsetoday.co.uk/news/regulation/bma-advising-gmc-on-update-to-written-reflections-guidance/)). The GMC, AoMRC, COPMeD and MSC responded with "The Reflective Practitioner" guidance in 2018 ([MDDUS](https://www.mddus.com/resources/resource-library/news-digest/2018/september/gmc-provides-guidance-on-reflective-practice)). Clinician ownership does not change this. A git-backed design makes it harder, because every committed version of a reflection persists in history. A clinician who later edits out identifiable or self-incriminating detail has not removed it, and a disclosure order could reach the whole repository. The product needs a deliberate policy on history for reflections, with clear user-facing wording. One option is to keep reflections outside the versioned tree or to allow history rewriting.

**NDG guidance on patient data in reflection.** The National Data Guardian's 3 December 2025 position accepts implied consent for regulated professionals reflecting on care they were involved in. It limits access to recent episodes (typically within about six months), applies a necessity test, recommends a brief note in the patient record and repeats that anonymised information should be used wherever possible ([NDG](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69303efdcdec734f4dff4198/NDG_postion_on_confidential_patent_data_use_in_reflective_practice_03.12.25.pdf)). For Clinician Passport this means anonymisation by default, prompts that discourage identifiers and, if AI drafting is added, automatic redaction before any text leaves the user's record. Under Quill's clinical safety process, identifiable data in a personal repository should appear in the hazard log and the DPIA.

**Single-source and unverified prices.** All 2026 GP appraisal prices (FourteenFish appraisal, Clarity, GP Tools, Umbil, BMJ Portfolio, GPnotebook) come from iatroX, a competitor, and need confirmation from vendor pages or sales contacts before they appear in any external material. The Revalidation Copilot competitor list is vendor-authored. The Australian college prices date from June 2023. L2P's individual subscription, QuietMedical's paid tier, Locum's Nest, Credentially, MyProgress and PebblePad prices were not retrieved. The research also found no published data quantifying data loss on leaving training or duplicate entry across appraisal, recruitment and agencies, so the core portability pain point rests on reasoning and anecdote and still needs primary validation through user interviews.

## Conclusion

The research supports building the product and is sceptical of the consumer subscription. Doctors who move between employers, especially SAS, locally employed and locum doctors, have a real, unclaimed need for a structured record they own, and L2P's paid "continuing" subscription shows vendors can already charge for continuity after leaving an organisation. Every comparable direct-to-clinician price, though, sits between about £15 and £50 a year, and the sustainable models elsewhere (Compassly, Patchwork, risr/) are paid for by institutions. A defensible starting position would be £9.99 a quarter or an annual equivalent for individual doctors, with viewing and export free indefinitely. The product would earn its keep through assessor sign-off workflows and framework hosting sold to trusts or specialty societies, using the Cheltenham oncology cohort as the reference site.

Two design decisions matter more than the price. The first is how the git history treats reflections, given that reflections have no legal privilege and the NDG expects anonymisation. The second is whether import from PDF exports out of Kaizen, Horus, ISCP and appraisal systems is good enough to make the "enter once, reuse everywhere" promise credible. Both should be settled before the free period ends and paying users arrive.
